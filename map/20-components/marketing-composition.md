# Marketing composition and content

Review marketing as a complete audience journey before judging individual section silhouettes. The `reading-first-landing` recipe in [compositions.tsx](../../catalogue/compositions.tsx) uses a generic workshop guide: invitation, preparation, checklist, discussion, and a return to a useful action. It lives in the recipe authority because no individual Component owns that complete journey. Its render and adaptable source consume the same structured content; the viewer remains independent.

Use MarketingSection's existing frame, spacing, and surface contracts to pace a page. Consecutive passages on the same surface can share a join; the preparation introduction removes its starting padding after the opener. A sunken checklist groups actionable material, while a contrast closing separates the final invitation. Every call to action resolves to content in the example rather than an imaginary product route.

A [Site header](../../src/components/marketing/site-header/site-header.tsx) navigation item may declare how it relates to the page being read: `current: "page"` for the exact destination and `current: "section"` for the branch containing it. The package keeps the anchor and renders `aria-current`; the consumer decides which item matches, because route shape, trailing slashes, and section membership are the consumer's own facts and the package carries no router. The current treatment styles `aria-current` directly rather than a private class, so the visual and assistive contracts cannot drift apart. The exact page carries a solid accent rule and its section a quiet dotted one, so neither relationship rests on colour alone.

HeroBlock and MarketingIntro use the independent marketing font role; EditorialHero retains the selected serif editorial voice. They size large titles against their local allocation. Semantic heading rank remains independent of visual scale. Titles balance ordinary lines and break otherwise unbreakable words; no fixed height or clipping carries the typography contract. Shared Section and type Tokens remain the authorities for global rhythm.

FeatureBento keeps its strict, complete rectangular footprint contract in [feature-bento-layout.ts](../../src/components/marketing/feature-bento/feature-bento-layout.ts). Choose footprints for the story: tall and large items need enough evidence to earn their span. Rows follow intrinsic content instead of imposing a minimum poster height; a wide item divides copy and visual only when the visual exists. The narrow layout follows source order. Unrelated item heights still belong to Grid or Masonry. AudienceGrid similarly lets short content stay short and lets its available cards fill the row.

[Marketing browser checks](../../tests/marketing_composition_browser_test.tsx) cover long titles, sparse cards, optional visual tracks, the complete page and canonical bento content at local and real page widths, doubled text, and fallback fonts. Existing matrix tests guard complete footprints; the Catalogue contact sheet remains the perceptual review surface, not a pixel-approval gate.

Product openings use the [material and voice rules](material-presence.md): establish a recognisable benefit before detailed mechanisms. HeroBlock `statement` and MarketingSection `compact` support concise software-product composition with breathing room. The [three-purpose specimen](../../catalogue/compositions/signature-specimens.tsx), available through Catalogue Compositions, and [minimal consumer](../../scripts/signature-consumer.tsx) demonstrate the selected rules with generic content. Detailed diagrams, terminals and transcripts belong after the visitor knows why to care.
