# Marketing composition and content

Review marketing as a complete audience journey before judging individual section silhouettes. The `reading-first-landing` recipe in [compositions.tsx](../../catalogue/compositions.tsx) uses a generic workshop guide: invitation, preparation, checklist, discussion, and a return to a useful action. It lives in the recipe authority because no individual Component owns that complete journey. Its render and adaptable source consume the same structured content; the viewer remains independent.

Use MarketingSection's existing frame, spacing, and surface contracts to pace a page. Section blocks (Audience grid, Feature bento, Split feature, Process steps, Metrics band, Comparison table, FAQ block, and the reading-first chapters) render on [MarketingSection](../../src/components/marketing/marketing-section/marketing-section.tsx), and every block with an ordinary section heading heads it with [MarketingIntro](../../src/components/marketing/marketing-intro/marketing-intro.tsx), so section padding, surfaces, the frame, title scale and tracking, and the eyebrow have one owner each; a block keeps only its own layout. EditorialHero and VoiceBreak keep their own editorial display type. Case study (an article) and CTA band (a card in its own gutter) keep their own frame but share MarketingIntro. Every text eyebrow is a [Kicker](../../src/components/display/kicker/kicker.tsx), the only uppercase label in the group; Hero block passes a node eyebrow, such as a Badge, through as given. Captions, metadata, and stat labels stay sentence case on `--discern-font-label` in muted ink, so the value-and-label unit reads the same in Case study, Metrics band, Outcome spotlight, and Testimonial. Numbers that align or compare (stats, metric values, step markers, counters) set tabular lining figures. Action rows share one rule, `.discern-marketing-actions` (composed through `marketingActionsClassName()`): actions stay on one line while they fit and each takes the full row once they wrap, so stacked actions share one width in every hero, CTA band, closing statement, and split feature. The reassurance or metadata line beneath an action row has one treatment too, `.discern-marketing-note` (`marketingNoteClassName()`): small muted UI-face text at body leading in Hero block, Editorial hero, CTA band, and Closing statement, each block keeping only its spacing. Testimonial and Voice break hang the same opening quote mark as Pull quote: the display face at one and a half times the large display size, accent-400 ink, and a 0.8 line height.

The contrast surface is one scope, `.discern-marketing-contrast`, owned by MarketingSection and composed through `marketingContrastClassName()` by any block that paints its own dark panel, such as CTA band's contrast tone. It remaps canvas, surface, ink, edges, the action roles, and the whole accent ladder together from the stable inverse pigments, so a primary Button turns light-on-dark, a secondary keeps its edge, and hover and press washes step up an opaque dark ladder (accent-100 above surface, then 200 and 300) whatever the page Accent; sunken insets sit below the canvas. Soft and deep tone roles are remapped too, so a success Badge reads as a dark chip with light ink; the base success, warning, and danger roles keep their page values, because a scope cannot remap a role from itself. Its canvas sits just above the inverse surface, and a contrast section draws hairline block edges clipped outside its own fill, so the chapter separates from a dark page without a light rim on a light one. In dark mode the chapter still reads darker than an ordinary surface section; lifting it needs a theme-aware Token rather than component CSS. [marketing_frame_test.tsx](../../tests/marketing_frame_test.tsx) fails a ladder or soft-tone role the scope leaves at its page value or remaps to a translucent wash. MarketingIntro's `tone="contrast"` sets light ink alone for a dark ground the consumer paints; inside the scope the default tone already reads light.

The accent ground is one rule as well: `.discern-marketing-accent` (`marketingAccentClassName()`, or MarketingSection `surface="accent"`) mixes the subtlest accent wash with the canvas and paints it over its own canvas. Hero block `surface="accent"`, Metrics band `tone="accent"`, and the Site header notice compose it. Under a dark Accent the full-strength wash is far brighter than the surface, so raised cards, windows, and secondary Buttons would read as holes cut into it; the tempered ground stays at or below the surface in every appearance, which a browser check proves. Sunken grounds (Section, MarketingSection, Hero block, and the inset Marketing stage) likewise paint over their own canvas, because the sunken wash is translucent. A well nested inside a raised surface (the Case study media, a Feature bento visual, a Testimonial avatar) uses `--discern-color-inset`, which sits just below its surface in both themes.

Every Marketing block that centres its own content lays out against the editorial page measure (`--discern-page-max`) and accepts `frame="wide"` to share the campaign frame (`--discern-page-max-wide`) with the campaign Site header, so a campaign page aligns its blocks without re-pointing the page token. Each block reads its measure through its own property, such as `--discern-hero-block-max` or MarketingSection's `--discern-marketing-section-max`, so a wide block never widens a block nested inside it. Hero block's showcase layout, Logo cloud's strip, and Site header's campaign variant default to wide. Logo cloud also offers `fill` for a cloud set inside another block's slot: it takes the slot's width without its own gutter or block padding. At a narrow allocation a start-aligned strip centres its label and spreads its marks into even columns, because a ragged left-aligned wrap reads as unfinished on a phone. The shared vocabulary lives in [frame.ts](../../src/components/marketing/frame.ts). [marketing_frame_test.tsx](../../tests/marketing_frame_test.tsx) fails a block that frames itself at the page measure without offering the wide frame, a frame control that never reaches the frame laying the block out, any stylesheet that writes a page-width token's value as a literal, and any Marketing stylesheet that hand-rolls an uppercase label, a dashed ornament, or a numeric weight. Consecutive passages on the same surface can share a join; the preparation introduction removes its starting padding after the opener. A sunken checklist groups actionable material, while a contrast closing separates the final invitation. Every call to action resolves to content in the example rather than an imaginary product route.

A [Site header](../../src/components/marketing/site-header/site-header.tsx) navigation item may declare how it relates to the page being read: `current: "page"` for the exact destination and `current: "section"` for the branch containing it. The package keeps the anchor and renders `aria-current`; the consumer decides which item matches, because route shape, trailing slashes, and section membership are the consumer's own facts and the package carries no router. The current treatment styles `aria-current` directly rather than a private class, so the visual and assistive contracts cannot drift apart. The exact page carries a 0.125rem accent-600 bar under its label, as a selected Tab does, and its section a faint hairline, so neither relationship rests on colour alone; hover is a half-strength accent wash and press the accent-200 fill, so hover never outshouts the current page. The navigation is an [Overflow cue](../../src/components/layout/overflow-cue/overflow-cue.tsx) descendant target: in a narrow header it becomes a full-bleed scroll row whose first label aligns with the brand, with room inside the row for every link's focus ring and hover fill, and a fade at the row's end shows that more links follow. `--discern-site-header-height` sets the header's row height and the Hero block's viewport-filling minimum together. The header and footer lockups compose the core [Brand](../../src/components/core/brand/brand.tsx), so mark treatments and typefaces have one owner.

[Site footer](../../src/components/marketing/site-footer/site-footer.tsx) lays its navigation groups out in an auto-fitting grid beside the brand column. `columns` asks for that many groups on one row. The brand and navigation share a row while that many minimum-width columns fit beside the brand; otherwise the navigation moves beneath the brand and takes the full width, and fewer columns form only when even that is too narrow, so the count never forces overflow. The count travels as `--discern-site-footer-columns`, which a stylesheet may also set.

HeroBlock and MarketingIntro use the independent marketing font role; EditorialHero retains the selected serif editorial voice. They size large titles against their local allocation. Semantic heading rank remains independent of visual scale. Titles balance ordinary lines and break otherwise unbreakable words; no fixed height or clipping carries the typography contract. Shared Section and type Tokens remain the authorities for global rhythm.

FeatureBento keeps its strict, complete rectangular footprint contract in [feature-bento-layout.ts](../../src/components/marketing/feature-bento/feature-bento-layout.ts). Choose footprints for the story: tall and large items need enough evidence to earn their span. Rows follow intrinsic content instead of imposing a minimum poster height; a wide item divides copy and visual only when the visual exists. An item with `align: "end"` keeps its icon at the top and sets its copy against the tile's end, for a tall or large tile whose copy should close the tile rather than hang beneath the icon; the icon's own margin remains the least space between them. The narrow layout follows source order. Unrelated item heights still belong to Grid or Masonry. AudienceGrid similarly lets short content stay short and lets its available cards fill the row. A featured audience card and an accent bento tile lift with a strong edge and the material highlight over their surface, keeping the card shadow, so the emphasis survives dark mode where shadows vanish; a sunken bento tile is flat. In the single-column stack every tile shares one copy inset, and a wide tile keeps at least 14rem for its copy beside a visual.

[Process steps](../../src/components/marketing/process-steps/process-steps.tsx) accepts an optional per-step `status`, named as the terminal stepper names it: `complete` draws the check glyph, `active` fills the marker with the action role and carries `aria-current="step"`, and `error` draws the warning mark on a danger ring with the detail line in ink; the step's detail wording names the state for every reader. Connectors leave equal room at both markers. [Comparison table](../../src/components/marketing/comparison-table/comparison-table.tsx) separates its header with a firm rule on the table surface and renders a text recommendation as an accent [Badge](../../src/components/display/badge/badge.tsx); when rows stack and the header hides, the badge text joins each highlighted cell's label, so the recommendation keeps its words. [Journey overview](../../src/components/marketing/journey-overview/journey-overview.tsx) steps share one subgrid, so titles, descriptions, and outcome rules start level across a row.

[Marketing browser checks](../../tests/marketing_composition_browser_test.tsx) cover long titles, sparse cards, optional visual tracks, the complete page and canonical bento content at local and real page widths, doubled text, fallback fonts, primary and secondary actions keeping their material on every contrast scope in both themes, a visible secondary hover, a dark Icon button press and legible Metrics band figures inside those scopes, the narrow Site header's focus rings, accent grounds that stay below the surfaces raised on them, nested wells below their surface, a centred Hero title that keeps a two-line measure, and FAQ answers that keep focus rings and settle at once under reduced motion. [reduced_motion_reach_test.ts](../../tests/reduced_motion_reach_test.ts) fails any Component pseudo-element other than `::before` and `::after` that moves without settling in its own reduced-motion block, because the foundation rule cannot reach it. Existing matrix tests guard complete footprints; the Catalogue contact sheet remains the perceptual review surface, not a pixel-approval gate.

Product openings use the [material and voice rules](material-presence.md): establish a recognisable benefit before detailed mechanisms. HeroBlock `statement` and MarketingSection `compact` support concise software-product composition with breathing room. A statement hero rules its visual slot off from the opening copy, unless a backdrop already carries the artwork's own lines through the block. The [three-purpose specimen](../../catalogue/compositions/signature-specimens.tsx), available through Catalogue Compositions, and [minimal consumer](../../scripts/signature-consumer.tsx) demonstrate the selected rules with generic content. Detailed diagrams, terminals and transcripts belong after the visitor knows why to care.
